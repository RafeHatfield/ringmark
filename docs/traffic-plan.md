## What you need to post (your accounts, your voice)

### 1. Reddit — highest priority

Two communities, same post. 120K + 85K members, exactly the right audience. Must come from a personal account that has some history in woodturning communities — don't post from a brand-new account.

**r/turning** — reddit.com/r/turning  
**r/woodturning** — reddit.com/r/woodturning

**Draft post:**

> **Title:** I built a way to give my bowls their full story — here's what it looks like
>
> I've been woodturning for [X] years and I always hated that once a piece leaves my shop, everything I know about it — where the wood came from, what I noticed when I cut into it, how long it dried — stays in my head or a notebook.
>
> So I built a thing. I take a quick photo at each stage, write a sentence or two, and at the end I generate a QR code. When someone buys the piece and scans it, they see the whole journey.
>
> Here's a maple burl I just finished: **ringmark.org/p/Z4ApFckA**
>
> Five stages from the Stanley Park log to the finished bowl, including the moment I cut into it and the figure appeared.
>
> Happy to share more about how it works if anyone's curious. Wondering if others have found good ways to do something similar.

Post these a few days apart. Lead with the work, not the tool. The piece page sells it.

### 2. AAW Forum — aawforum.org

The most serious dedicated woodturning forum online (~15K members). Post in the Technology or Show Us Your Work section. Same angle as Reddit — you're a woodturner sharing something you built, not a product launching.

### 3. Creator outreach — handle personally

**Nick Zammeti** — 892K YouTube, large Instagram. Biggest dedicated woodturning creator. DM or email via his site. Don't ask for promotion upfront — ask for feedback. "I'm a woodturner who built a provenance tool, curious what you think of this piece page." If he reacts to it in a video, that's the ideal outcome.

**Ashley Harwood** — 95K YouTube, US-based, community and teaching focus. Better organic fit than Nick for content about the meaning and business side of woodturning.

**American Woodturner (AAW journal)** — Pitch a short article, not a product feature. Angle: *"How QR codes and digital provenance are changing how woodturners connect with buyers."* Tell the story of a specific piece (the maple burl). Contact via woodturner.org. They cover technology and craft stories.

## Submissions you can do (no account needed from me)

### Product Hunt

Free submission. DR 91 backlink. Gets indexed quickly. Best posted Tuesday–Thursday. The craft/maker audience is thin there but the SEO value is real and it occasionally gets picked up by newsletters.

**Copy ready to paste:**

> **Name:** Ringmark  
> **Tagline:** Track the story of wood from log to finished piece  
> **Topics:** Maker Tools · Productivity · Small Business  
>
> **Description:**  
> Ringmark is a workshop tracking tool for woodturners and craft makers. You photograph a piece at every stage — from the raw log to the finished bowl — write a short note at each step, then generate a QR code. When a buyer scans it, they see the full journey: where the wood came from, how it was processed, who made it, and why.
>
> It's built for the moment you hand someone a handmade bowl and they ask "where did this wood come from?" Now the bowl can answer.
>
> Built by a woodturner, for woodturners. Free to try — get in touch at ringmark.org.
